HOSTING A CO-OP GAME
The SpongeBob SquarePants Movie, PC port: online co-op for two players

This guide is for the player who hosts: the other player joins your game.
The other player has their own guide, JOINING.txt.

You need to do steps 1 to 3 once. After that, hosting takes a few seconds.


SAME HOME NETWORK? START HERE
-----------------------------
If the other player's PC, phone or Switch (with the co-op version of the
game on it) is on the same Wi-Fi or home network as you, you need none of
the steps below: in the Co-op tab, press
"Host on this network". Your game shows a join code in large letters, the
other device finds your game in its list, and its player types the code.
LAN.txt explains it. The rest of this guide is for playing over the internet.


THE EASY WAY: THROUGH THE CO-OP SERVER
--------------------------------------
No router settings and no IP address are needed. Both games connect to a
co-op server, which puts you in one room.

1. Start the game, open the overlay (F1, or hold Back and press Start on a
   controller) and go to the Co-op tab.
2. Check the "Co-op server" box (it is filled in already) and press
   "Host on the server".
3. The tab shows a room code (4 characters) and a join code. Give both to the
   other player in person or in a message.

The other player types both codes (JOINING.txt). The join code stays between
the two of you: the server cannot read your game. If the other player leaves,
the room stays open. If you stop hosting, the room closes.
The rest of this guide is the other way, with a forwarded port, for when you
have no co-op server.


WHAT HOSTING MEANS
------------------
Your game runs the world for both of you: the enemies, the moving platforms,
the pickups, the level changes and the cutscenes. Your save is the one that
counts. The other player's game connects straight to yours over the
internet. There is no server in between and no account to make.

For that, your router has to let the other player's game reach your PC. That
is what "forwarding a port" means (step 2). Only the host does this. The
player who joins does not need to change anything on their router.


1. FIND YOUR PC'S LOCAL ADDRESS
-------------------------------
Start the game. Open the overlay with F1 (on a controller: hold Back and
press Start). Go to the Co-op tab.

  What you see: the tab says "Online co-op", "Status: Off", your name, then
  "Join a game" with the list of games on this network, and "Host a game"
  with two buttons, "Host on this network" and "Host over the internet".

Type your name (it is shown above your head on the other player's screen).
Press "Host over the internet". Leave the UDP port at 2171 unless you have a
reason to change it. Leave "Use a join code" ticked. Press "Start hosting".

  What you see: "Status: Waiting for a player", your join code in large
  letters (three groups of four letters and digits, for example
  K7QX-M2PD-9TRW), "Hosting on UDP port 2171." and a line such as
  "Over the internet: on your router, forward UDP port 2171 to this device,
  192.168.1.23."

That 192.168.1.23 (yours will differ) is your PC's local address. Write it
down for step 2.

The first time you press "Start hosting", Windows may ask about the firewall
(step 3). You can answer it now.


2. FORWARD THE PORT ON YOUR ROUTER
----------------------------------
Every router looks different, but the steps are the same.

a) Open your router's settings page in a web browser. Its address is often
   printed on a sticker on the router, usually 192.168.0.1, 192.168.1.1 or
   10.0.0.1. Log in with the router's admin password (also often on the
   sticker, or in the router's app).

     What you see: the router maker's page, with menus such as "Status",
     "Wireless", "Advanced" or "Network".

b) Find the port forwarding section. It may be called "Port Forwarding",
   "Port Forward", "Virtual Servers", "NAT Forwarding", "Applications and
   Gaming" or "Firewall > Port rules". It is often under "Advanced".

     What you see: a list of rules (maybe empty) and a button such as "Add",
     "Add Rule", "Create" or "+".

c) Add a rule with these values:
     Name or service:        TSSM co-op (any name you like)
     Protocol:               UDP  (not TCP; "TCP/UDP" or "Both" also works)
     External port (start and end, or "public port"):  2171
     Internal port (start and end, or "private port"): 2171
     Internal IP / device / server IP:  the local address from step 1,
                                        for example 192.168.1.23
   If the router asks for a device instead of an address, pick your PC from
   the list. Turn the rule on ("Enabled") and save or apply it.

     What you see: the new rule in the list, for example
     "TSSM co-op   UDP   2171   192.168.1.23   Enabled".

d) Keep your PC's local address the same. Routers sometimes give a PC a new
   local address after a restart, and then the rule points at the wrong
   place. Most routers have "DHCP reservation", "Address reservation" or
   "Always use this IP address" next to the list of devices: turn it on for
   your PC. (If you skip this, check the address in the Co-op tab each time
   and fix the rule when it changes.)

The game never changes your router by itself. Only you do, here.


3. LET THE GAME THROUGH WINDOWS FIREWALL
---------------------------------------
The first time you press "Start hosting", Windows may show a window titled
"Windows Security" saying that Windows Defender Firewall has blocked some
features of this app, naming tssm.exe.

  What you see: two tick boxes, "Private networks, such as my home or work
  network" and "Public networks, such as those in airports and coffee
  shops", and the buttons "Allow access" and "Cancel".

Tick "Private networks" (your home network). Leave "Public networks"
unticked unless your PC really is on a public network. Press "Allow access".

If you pressed Cancel by mistake, you can fix it yourself: open the Start
menu, type "Allow an app through Windows Firewall" and open it, press
"Change settings", find tssm.exe in the list (or press "Allow another app"
and pick it), tick "Private" and press OK.

The game never changes your firewall by itself. Only you do, here.


4. FIND YOUR PUBLIC ADDRESS
---------------------------
The other player needs your public address: the address of your home
internet connection, not the 192.168.x.x one. Two ways to find it:

- Your router's status page (often the first page after logging in) shows
  it as "WAN IP", "Internet IP", "Public IP" or "IPv4 address" under
  "Internet".
- Or search the web for "what is my IP" in your browser.

It looks like 203.0.113.7 (four numbers with dots).

If the router's "WAN IP" starts with 100.64 to 100.127, 10., 172.16 to
172.31, or 192.168., and is different from what the web search shows, your
internet provider shares one public address among many homes (this is
called "CGNAT"). Port forwarding then cannot work. Ask your provider for a
public IPv4 address (some do it for free when asked), or let the other
player host instead.

Your public address can change from time to time (for example when the
router restarts). Check it again if joining stops working.


5. HOST AND SHARE THE JOIN CODE
-------------------------------
Load your save (or start a new game) and play in the level you want to play
together: the other player's game comes to your level by itself. Press
"Start hosting" in the Co-op tab (the game also remembers your name and port
for next time). Send the other player, in a message or by phone:

  - your public address and the port, written as 203.0.113.7:2171
  - the join code shown in the Co-op tab, for example K7QX-M2PD-9TRW
    ("Copy" puts it on the clipboard). Capitals, spaces and dashes do not
    matter when they type it.

The join code stays the same until you press "New code" beside it (or
start the game again); press it for a fresh code, for example when someone
you did not invite may have seen it. You can instead untick "Use a join code" and type a password of
your own (at least 8 characters is wise). The game never saves the password
or the code to a file.

When the other player is in, the status says "Connected", their name and
ping appear under "Partner", and a short notice says "a player joined your
game".


WHAT TO TELL THE OTHER PLAYER
-----------------------------
- Install the same version of the port as yours. Both Co-op tabs show a
  "Co-op version" code at the bottom: the two codes must be the same.
  32-bit and 64-bit builds of the same version play together.
- Use their own copy of the game files (from their own copy of the game,
  the same release and region as yours), with the same mods turned on as
  yours, or none. Never send them your game files.
- Join with your public address and port (203.0.113.7:2171) and the join
  code. JOINING.txt walks them through it.


TESTING AT HOME FIRST
---------------------
On the same home network you do not need port forwarding: tick "Visible on
this network" in your Co-op tab while hosting, and the other PC finds your
game in its list (LAN.txt), or joins with your local address from step 1,
for example 192.168.1.23:2171. If that works but joining over the internet
does not, the problem is the port forward (step 2) or the public address
(step 4).


WHILE YOU PLAY
--------------
- You lead: when you go to another level, the other player's game comes
  along, and both of you start the level together. Your cutscenes play on
  both games, and only you can skip them.
- Only your game saves, and your save keeps everything the two of you do
  together: the tokens, shiny objects and moves the other player gets count
  in it. The other player's game does not write its save while it is
  joined, and their own save keeps nothing from the session.
- Each of you picks a character: tap LB (Z on the keyboard) to switch
  between SpongeBob and Patrick, hold it to change your costume. Both of you
  may play the same character. The Co-op tab's "Customize" section does the
  same.
- Top right of the screen, a coloured dot and a number show the connection:
  green is good, yellow is fair, red is poor, and the number is the ping in
  milliseconds (the time a message takes there and back). "no signal" means
  nothing has come from the other game for a moment.
- If the other player's window is held or their game stalls, your screen
  says "waiting for NAME's game"; it picks up again by itself when theirs
  runs again.
- If the connection drops, the other player's game joins again by itself for
  up to a minute. You do not need to do anything; the join code stays the
  same as long as you keep hosting.
- "Kick" (in the Co-op tab, while someone is connected) removes the other
  player and blocks their address until you stop hosting.
- "Stop hosting" ends it.


WHEN SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-------------------------
The other player's game tells them why it could not join. What to check:

"No answer from the host"
    - Are you hosting right now (Co-op tab: "Waiting for a player")?
    - Did they type your public address (not 192.168...) and the right port?
    - Does the router rule forward UDP (not only TCP) port 2171 to your PC's
      current local address (step 1 shows it)?
    - Did you allow tssm.exe through Windows Firewall (step 3)?
    - Is your connection behind CGNAT (step 4)?
"Wrong join code or password"
    Send the code again, as your Co-op tab shows it now.
"The host's game is a different version"
    You need the same version of the port: compare the "Co-op version" at
    the bottom of both Co-op tabs. This is what you see when one of you
    still runs an older co-op zip: both switch to the newest one.
"Your game files differ from the host's"
    You need the same release and region of the game, and the same mods on
    (easiest: both turn mods off in the Mods tab). Compare the "Game files"
    code at the bottom of both Co-op tabs.
"The host's game already has a player"
    Co-op is for two. Kick the other player first, or stop and start hosting.
"Blocked" or "refusing joins for a minute"
    Too many wrong codes were tried. Wait a minute (and check the code).

"Host over the internet" hosts with "Visible on this network" unticked:
devices on your own network do not see the game in their list until you tick
it. "Host on this network" ticks it.

Your Co-op tab counts wrong password attempts. If you see many you did not
expect, stop hosting: someone is guessing. Press "New code" before you host
again.


SAFETY
------
- The game only sends game state (positions, animations, what happens in
  the level) and checks everything it receives. It never sends files, and
  it does not let the other player change your save.
- The join code is checked without ever being sent over the internet.
  Traffic is protected against tampering but not encrypted: do not type
  anything private as your name.
- Remove the port forwarding rule if you stop playing co-op for good.
